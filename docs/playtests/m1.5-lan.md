## What this establishes

The first time two humans were in one session, over a real socket, on a real
host process rather than the in-page loopback, the combat felt acceptable to
the person judging it. That is the half of R2 that T-1.24 structurally could
not reach (`docs/playtests/m1.md`, "Two humans' inputs interacting"): the
sparring bot never shoots, so nothing before this exercised two people
shooting each other under lag compensation.

It also establishes the plumbing: two people got into the same room through
the lobby and the join-code path (T-1.5.04 through T-1.5.06), which is what
the M1.5 acceptance bar in `docs/HANDOFF-M1.5.md` §1 asks for.

## What it does not establish

Required by T-1.5.03's own wording rather than optional.

**The individual cases the task named are not individually recorded.** The
task asked for a verdict naming which of these feel fair and which do not:

- two players contesting one doorway;
- each shooting the other inside the same 200 ms rewind window;
- whether "I shot first" disputes resolve in a way *both* people accept;
- whether a corpse taking no further damage reads as correct or as a
  swallowed hit.

The sign-off is a summary across the session. Which of these were played, and
what each felt like, cannot be reconstructed from it. If any one of them is
wanted on the record it must be re-played with intent, and the note taken
while it is fresh.

**Conditioning levels are not recorded.** The task asked for LAN, then the
host's conditioning at 80 ms and 200 ms, with the two players' conditions set
independently. Whether any conditioned run happened is not in the record.
The verdict should be read as "at LAN" and nothing more.

**"Both people accept" is one person's report.** The second player's view is
not separately recorded.

**What a LAN leaves unproven regardless**, per the task: NAT traversal and
connection establishment against a remote host; real, heavy-tailed jitter
distributions; routing; and any latency a slider did not put there. Those are
T-1.5.08's to answer (`m1.5.md`).
