## What this is judging

T-3.10 through T-3.23 built the enemy: it sees and hears (T-3.13, T-3.14),
fires through the same authoritative path a human's shot takes (T-3.15),
suppresses and is suppressed (T-3.16, T-3.17), takes cover it chose
(T-3.18–T-3.20), pins and flanks as a group (T-3.21), throws grenades at a
soldier who camps (T-3.22), and comes in two archetypes, the rifleman and
the MG (T-3.23). Each is held to numbers headless (`pnpm sim-run --scenario
cover-duel`, `pinned`, `mg`); this gate is whether it *plays* right. The
questions the gate asks (PLAN.md T-3.24):

1. Do enemies take cover in a way that reads as **a decision rather than a
   coincidence**?
2. Does **the MG pin you**?
3. Does being flanked feel like **being outplayed or like being cheated**?
4. Does **a grenade come when you camp**?
5. Can you **tell an enemy from a squadmate at 40 m**?

And: tune the archetype data while the feel is in hand.

## Before you start

- **Controls.** `WASD` move, `Shift` sprint, `C` crouch, `Z` prone, `Space`
  jump, `RMB` aim, `LMB` fire, `R` reload, `1`–`4` guns (carbine, marksman,
  breacher, sidearm), `5`–`6` the pouch (frag, rocket; `LMB` throws what is
  in hand), `E` held revives. `B` toggles the AI debug overlay, `N` the
  netgraph, `H` the HUD, `Tab` the scoreboard, `T` resets the counters,
  `Esc` pauses (for you only; the session runs on). `Q` held is the order
  wheel and `F` marks — used here only to park the bots (below).
- **The overlay is this sheet's instrument.** `B` draws, over each brain,
  `bot <netId>`, the branch of its tree that is running, where it was told
  to walk (`walk → x, z` / `sprint → …`) and the path it is walking.
  Enemies are netIds 2000 and up; squad slots 1–6 are netIds 1–6. Leaf
  names to look for: `takeCover`, `peek`, `hide`, `reload`, `advance`,
  `engageOpen`, `suppress`, `flank`, `throwGrenade`, `atEase`. **It shows
  no view cones, known targets or chosen cover point** — the report sends
  those empty (`server/src/ai/debug.ts`); that is a gap in the tool, not
  in the AI. On the host the overlay needs `AI_DEBUG=1`; without it, the
  status line reads "AI debug: waiting for the host (a remote host needs
  AI_DEBUG=1)". The in-page session always answers.
- **Where the MG is.** Nothing puts an MG on **the range** on a host: the
  range has no encounter, and `?enemies` spawns riflemen only
  (`client/src/net/qaEnemies.ts`). The only mixed group in this build is
  the grey-box map's **garrison** (`data/encounters/greybox-01.json`: three
  riflemen and an MG, one group, holding the objective compound). At one
  or two humans the director scales it to **two riflemen and the MG**
  (`data/director.json` budget 0.5 / 0.6, rounded, never below one). So
  the gate is played on `greybox-01`, not the range; say so in the verdict.
- **The numbers you are judging** (defaults; all data, not code):

  | | rifleman | MG |

  | health, downable | 100, no | 120, no |
  | weapon (`weapons.json`) | carbine: 720 rpm, 30 rounds, 2.1 s reload | lmg: 900 rpm, 100-round belt, 4.5 s reload |

  From `data/enemies.json`, `data/trees/rifleman.json` and `trees/mg.json`.
  Both see 80 m in a 120° cone; detection builds up rather than flipping
  on (measured at T-3.13: 0.70 / 1.17 / 2.77 s standing / crouched / prone
  at 40 m). A rifleman advances after 5 s unopposed, never inside 12 m.
  **Group** (`server/src/ai/group.json`): pinned once nobody in it has
  seen you for 1 s; the suppressor fires at your last known feet + 1.3 m
  without sight; the flanker takes a point 6–30 m from you, on a route that
  costs 8× per metre you could see. **Grenade** (`server/src/ai/throw.json`):
  you have stayed within 1 m for 3 s, 8–35 m away, low body hidden from it;
  one throw per 5 s per thrower; a frag has a 3.5 s fuse and 6.5 m blast
  (`projectiles.json`). **Suppression** (`data/suppression.json`): +0.2 per
  near miss (within 1 m of your capsule), held 0.75 s, then −0.35/s; at
  full it widens your cone by 1.5°.
- **Hearing is through walls.** A shot is heard inside its radius
  whatever is in between (T-3.14: occlusion is a later tuning pass), and it
  places the shooter. An enemy that turns toward you through a wall after
  you fire is working as built — note whether it *feels* like cheating.
- **Known, not for this gate:** B-09 — your crouched shot still leaves
  from standing eye height, so crouched behind the low wall you can shoot
  over it while hidden (enemies fire from their own eye in their stance;
  only yours is wrong). B-11 — friendly bots lose most mission fights;
  that is E-3.8's and the mission's. RPG, sniper and officer are valid in
  the schema and absent from the data.
- **Parking the squad.** With `HOST_AI=1` the four bots follow and fight.

  ahead, hold `Q`, move the mouse down-right (the **hold** sector), release.
  Every bot walks to that point and holds, firing only at what it sees.

## The questions

A line each is enough; "fine" is a valid answer, "did not try" is a
required one.

### 0. Warm-up, in the page, alone

`pnpm --filter @sandline/client dev`, open
`localhost:5173/?mission&world=greybox-01`, choose **Practise here**. That
is the same encounter in the page, with the bots standing idle on the
spawn line (no `&squad`), the overlay always available, and a JSON edit
picked up on reload — the place to tune. `P` restarts once it is won or
lost. Then `localhost:5173/?suppress` for the suppression look on its own:
one rifleman 25 m up the lane shooting at the squadmate beside you.

- `?suppress`: does the vignette, desaturation and jolt read as "rounds are
  going past me", or as a screen effect? Does the crosshair widening show?
- Anything in the page that stops you playing the host part?

### 1. Enemy or squadmate at 40 m (T-3.11, T-4.35)

Enemies wear the irregular fighter (kameez, pakol or turban; the MG gunner
a bandolier); the squad the desert-camouflage soldier (T-4.08). On the
host, have the other person stand beside a bot 40 m from you, in the open,
then behind a low wall; walk an enemy into the same range if you can.

- At 40 m, in the open: enemy or squadmate, without the overlay? At what
  range does it stop being obvious? ______ m
- Crouched behind cover, only a head and shoulders showing: still?
- In shade, against the compound walls, against open ground?
- Did you ever shoot a squadmate, or hold fire on an enemy, because of
  how they looked? (`?codesoldier` gives the old code-built look, for
  comparison only.)

### 2. Cover — a decision or a coincidence? (T-3.19, T-3.20)

Approach the compound by either lane until the garrison sees you. With `B`
on, watch one rifleman from first contact.

- Does it go *to* cover, facing you, or does it happen to be near a wall?
  Seconds from first shot to in cover: ______ s (scenario floor: 5 s).
- Peeking: does the stand-up-and-burst over a low wall, or the side-step
  out of a high one, read as peeking? Does it go back down?
- Does it reload behind cover rather than in the open?
- Pressure it (sustained fire at its cover). Does it stay down while
  suppressed and come back up when you stop?
- Stop shooting and hide for 5 s. Does it advance, cover to cover, and
  does that read as it pushing you?
- Any cover choice that looked wrong — hiding on your side of a wall,
  standing in a doorway, shuffling between two points? Note the netId and
  the overlay's branch.

### 3. The MG (T-3.23)

Find the MG (overlay: the brain with `suppress` in its branch once the
group has pinned you; up close, the bandolier).

- Does it pin you — do you stay down behind cover while it fires, because
  you judge you must, not because the vignette tells you to?
- Its bursts (45 rounds): do they feel like an MG, or like a rifleman who
  never lets go? Is the 1.5 s deploy before its first round visible?
- Once you are flanking it: does it relocate rarely and badly (a walk, not
  a sprint), and is it then the thing worth killing first?
- Kill it with the marksman (`2`) from outside its cone's reach. Does the
  marksman feel like the answer to it (ADR-015)?

### 4. Flanked — outplayed or cheated? (T-3.21)

Both people hold cover and stay hidden for a couple of seconds so the group
pins you. Watch for `flank` in one brain's branch and `suppress` in
another's.

- Did the suppression keep you down while someone moved? Could you tell
  that is what was happening?
- The flanker's route: did it come round out of your sight, or walk across
  open ground in front of you?
- When it reached its point and fired at your side: did you get a chance to
  see it coming (a sound, a glimpse, a callout from the other person)? Did
  it feel earned — outplayed — or did it know where you were in a way you
  could not have avoided — cheated? Which way, and why?
- Did it ever flank through a wall, or appear where it could not have got
  to?

### 5. A grenade when you camp (T-3.22)

Crouch behind one piece of cover, 8–35 m from a rifleman that knows you,
and do not move more than a metre.

- Does a grenade come? After about ______ s (data: 3 s still, then the
  search).
- Could you see or hear it coming in time to move? Does moving out work?
- Did one ever land on its own side or on a friend of its own?
- Did a grenade ever come when you were moving, or in the open?

### 6. Over the host, both people

- Are enemies, their fire and their deaths the same on both screens, at
  the same moment? A corpse lies 10 s, then goes.
- Two targets: does the group split between you, or fixate on one? Does
  target choice feel fair (the visible, the close, the one shooting at it)?
- Restart the host with `LINK_LATENCY_MS=100 LINK_JITTER_MS=20
  LINK_LOSS=0.05` (the link panel's sliders grey out on a host). Anything
  that reads differently — enemies hitting you behind cover, peeks you
  never saw?
