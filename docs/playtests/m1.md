> "I have qa tested it, and it behaves great! T1.24 is complete."

M1's exit gate is met and M2 is open. **ADR-012 is not reopened** — the
condition for revisiting it was this gate failing, and it did not.

## What this establishes

An authoritative-server third-person shooter, predicted and reconciled in a
browser, feels good across the link conditions the harness can produce. That is
R2's question, and answering it before any content investment is the entire
reason M0 and M1 came first.

## What it does not establish

Required by T-1.24 rather than optional: a gate that overstates itself is worse
than a thin one.

**Outside the harness at any slider setting.** These need a real host:

- Real jitter distributions. NetSim draws uniform jitter about a mean; real
  networks are heavy-tailed.
- Reordering under congestion, and TCP head-of-line blocking.
- NAT traversal and connection establishment against a remote host.
- **Two humans' inputs interacting** — two people contesting the same doorway,
  or each landing a fatal shot on the other under lag compensation. The
  sparring partner never shoots, so nothing here exercises the path that "a
  corpse takes no further damage" exists to handle. This is the real gap, and
  the only one on this list that is about gameplay rather than transport.

**Not captured in this record.** The sign-off is a summary across what was
played, not a reading at LAN / 80-15-5 / 200-40-20 individually, and it does not
separately record the two link assignments the build offers — your own link
degraded against a LAN partner, versus your own held at LAN with the partner
degraded. Both were available. If that detail is wanted later it must be
re-played; it cannot be reconstructed from this.

**So a passing T-1.24 does not close R2.** It answers the question for one
player against a simulated link. Re-gate the two-human case when E-4.9 deploys
a real host, per ADR-012's addendum.
