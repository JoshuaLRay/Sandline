## What this is judging

E-2.7 made the game's sound in-house (ADR-017): every effect is a recipe
rendered offline by `pnpm gen:audio`, played by a Web Audio engine that
places, muffles and delays it (T-2.44, T-2.45); the guns' near and far
reports and their handling (T-2.46); rounds going past, impacts, grenades,
rockets, explosions, footsteps and bodies (T-2.47); a voice pipeline for
recorded callouts (T-2.48); and callouts in play (T-2.49). The gate's
questions (PLAN.md §7.10):

1. Can a shot's **direction and distance** be told by ear?
2. Do **walls muffle** what they should, and only that?
3. Do **callouts help or nag**?
4. Does it **read as 2002** — the period and the kind of game — or as
   something else?

**Nobody has recorded the voice script yet** (`docs/audio/voice-script.md`),
so every callout is the **radio chirp**: two short beeps. This sheet judges
when and how often the squad speaks, and whether it is heard from the right
place. It cannot judge the voices. If recordings have been processed by the
time you play, the sound board lists them; say which you heard.

## The questions

A line each is enough; "fine" is a valid answer, "did not try" is a
required one.

### 1. The sound board (T-2.44 – T-2.47)

Open `?sounds`. Play every variant of every sound at least once.

- Anything that is plainly broken — a click, silence, a sound cut off
  early, one much louder or quieter than its neighbours?
- The guns (`carbine-near` … `lmg-far`): does each sound like a different
  gun, and does each far version sound like the same gun a long way off?
- Handling (`reload-out`, `reload-in`, `reload-bolt`, `dry-fire`, `equip`):
  period-plausible, or toy-like?

### 2. Direction and distance by ear (T-2.45, T-2.46)

On the range with `?enemies` (three riflemen: one standing, two patrolling),
turn your back to the riflemen, close your eyes, and let them fire.

- Can you point at the shooter by ear — left, right, in front, behind?
  How far off are you, roughly?
- Near vs far: walk towards a firing rifleman from as far as the range
  allows. Does the report change from far to near smoothly, or can you hear
  the cross-fade?
- Your own gun: does your shot sound like yours and sit in front of you?
- Rounds past your head: stand near where the riflemen are shooting. Does
  the crack come from the side the round passed? Does it read as "that was
  close", or as a noise?
- A grenade (`G`) at a distance: is the blast heard after the flash when
  far, and at once when near? Can you tell the near from the far blast?

### 3. Walls (T-2.45)

Put a wall between you and a firing rifleman, then step out from behind it.

- Behind the wall: is the fire muffled? Does it sound behind a wall, or
  just quieter?
- Stepping out: does it open up at the moment you can see them?
- Does anything sound muffled that should not (a clear line of sight), or
  clear that should not (through a wall)?
- The low wall (1 m): stand, then crouch, behind it. Does crouching muffle
  it as it should? (The ray is from your camera to the source.)

### 4. Footsteps and bodies (T-2.47)

- Walk, sprint, crouch-walk and crawl (`Z`) on your own. Do the steps land
  with the feet? Do the four paces sound different?
- The patrolling riflemen: can you hear them walking before you see them?
  From how far?
- Jump off something and land; vault the low wall. Heard, and right?
- A soldier hit, going down, dying: does each read?

### 5. Callouts (T-2.49)

`?squad&enemies`: five squad bots with you against the riflemen. Let them
fight for two minutes; then give orders with the wheel (hold `Q`, pick,
release), including a move to somewhere unreachable (off the edge of the
range) for "can't get there".

- **Help or nag?** Count, roughly, the chirps in a minute of fighting:
  ______. Too many, too few, or about right?
- Near and far: a squadmate within 25 m is heard where they stand; one
  beyond it over the radio. Can you hear the difference? Is 25 m right?
- Only one voice on the radio at a time: does it ever sound like two at
  once, or does an important call (man down, grenade) get lost?
- Does your own soldier's reloading and grenade call help, or would you
  rather it were silent?
- Did an order's acknowledgement come every time you gave one? Did "can't
  get there" come for the unreachable move?
- With chirps only, is there any way to tell one callout from another? (If
  not, that is expected until recordings — say whether the timing alone
  still helps.)

### 6. The mission map (`?mission&squad`)

Play the grey-box mission once through.

- The same questions as 2, 3 and 5, on bigger ground: is anything harder to
  place, or too quiet at the distances the mission plays at?
- The mounted MG (T-4.29): mount it and fire. Its sound, and its equip?
- The objective callouts: "compound clear", "holding", "secure" — did each
  come when it should?
- Does the whole fight get too loud or too busy at any point? Where?

### 7. Two people over the host

Get a second person into a room with you. Stand apart, then together.

- Their shots, reloads, footsteps and grenades: heard, and from where they
  are?
- A squadmate's reload is heard from their body — can you tell they are
  reloading without looking?
- Anything you heard that they did not do, or that they did that you did
  not hear?
- Link to Poor, then Awful: do sounds arrive late, or double, or go
  missing?

### 8. Does it read as 2002?

- Does the whole thing sound like a squad shooter of that period — not
  modern cinematic, not arcade?
- What single change would make it most of all sound right?
