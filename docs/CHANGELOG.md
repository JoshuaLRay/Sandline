- B-01 — Fixed the strafing tracer bug: a shot's `HitEvent` now carries the shooter's own rewound origin (`originX/Y/Z`, PROTOCOL_VERSION 13), the same point `resolveShot` already traced from server-side, instead of leaving every OTHER client to approximate one from the shooter's current replicated position. That approximation lagged a strafing shooter's true muzzle by however far they moved during the round trip plus the interpolation delay — a small angle at range, but at close quarters the same absolute drift is a large fraction of the shot's own length, which is what read as a tracer running sideways rather than along the shooter's aim. Confirmed with two headless clients over a real host under 150 ms latency: the drawn origin tracked the shooter's actual position to the wire's quantization instead of trailing 1.5–2 m behind it.
- B-02 — Fixed the run-and-fire strobing bug: `LocalInput` gains a `bodyYaw` getter that is the mouse's own accumulated yaw with recoil's view kick (`offsetYaw`) left out, and `main.ts` now classifies the local player's locomotion against `bodyYaw` instead of `yaw`. - B-02 (reopened, refixed) — Reverted PR #44's `bodyYaw` change, which never fixed the run-and-fire strobe: the body it re-classified is hidden in first person, where the bug was reported. The real cause was the muzzle flash. Shots are taken on the tick, from the character's tick-end position, but the camera renders from a position interpolated up to a tick behind that; the aimed first-person muzzle is the eye, and the flash sits `FLASH_FORWARD_M` (8 cm) in front of it, just inside the 0.1 m near plane. Standing still, that keeps it clipped out every frame. Running forward or backward, the tick-to-frame gap runs straight down the view axis, so the flash landed in front of the near plane on some frames and behind it on others, and each round blinked a screen-filling sprite on or off. Strafing moves sideways, not along that axis, which is why it didn't strobe. Fix: `main.ts` queues each shot's flash and shell and draws them from the muzzle as it is drawn in that frame (the rendered position, at the camera's own pivot height). `WeaponEffects.followMuzzle` keeps live flashes on that muzzle for their two frames. Shells now inherit the shooter's velocity, so a shooter running backward can no longer overtake their own brass into the middle of the view. Hits are still traced from the tick's eye, and tracers are unchanged. `pnpm verify` green.
