### What was wrong (on `main`, 13995e9)

- **Your own sounds were placed in the world, at your own body.** `playOwn` plays at the tick's rig muzzle point (`muzzlePosition`), and every such sound went through a head-related (HRTF) panner at that point. The sounds affected: the gun report, dry fire, reload stages and equip. Footsteps, landing and the vault did the same at your feet.
- **From the hip, that point is directly to your right.** In first-person hipfire the point is the hip: 0.26 m right of the eye and 0.5 m below it, with no forward offset at all. Seen from the ear, that is straight right and a little down, so HRTF put your own gun almost entirely in the right ear.
- **Other views and stances fared differently.** At ADS the point is the eye itself, a degenerate direction at zero distance. In third person it is the shoulder, well ahead of the camera. So your own report also changed character with the view and the stance.

### Change

- **A data-driven own mix:** `mix.json` gains `own: { pan }`, schema-validated in [−1, 1] and set to 0.1.
- **What your own sound gets:** any sound played with the `own` flag, in a placed class, is now not placed in the world. It has:
  - no HRTF;
  - no distance falloff;
  - no occlusion;
  - no delay;
  - only a `StereoPannerNode` at `own.pan`.
- **The same whichever way you face:** stance, view, ADS and where the rig point happens to be make no difference.
- **Everyone else is unchanged:** their shots, footsteps, impacts and callouts keep HRTF placement, falloff, occlusion and delay (`place`, `spatial.ts`).
- **Unplaced sounds are unchanged:** the UI still has no panner.
- **Stereo, gain and clipping:** a mono render through an equal-power stereo pan at 0.1 gives 0.65 left and 0.76 right. Both are under unity, so no new clipping, and it sits in both ears instead of one. The bus chain (effects, then master, then destination) is unchanged, so the volume sliders apply as before.
- **Listener convention, pinned:** `listenerSide` states the convention the engine's listener uses: right is forward × up, with up +Y, as `setListener` sets it from the camera. The tests hold three.js's camera frame to it.

### Verification
