### Design

- **Rules as data** (`shared/src/data/pickups.json`, `sim/pickups.ts`, validated by name):
  - which weapons drop: the squad's primaries, carbine, marksman rifle and breacher;
  - `despawnSeconds`: 120;
  - `max` lying at once: 24.
- **Policy for what does not drop:**
  - the MG enemy's LMG, which is not a loadout weapon until U-020 (U-018 equips);
  - a sidearm, never a primary;
  - launchers, which are the pouch's;
  - an emplacement's gun, which stays on its mount. An enemy killed on a gun drops its own weapon, never the gun.
- **Exactly once per death.** `killEnemy` drops the weapon only for an enemy that is actually dead, guarded by a per-entity `dropped` flag:
  - a death seen again (the brain sweep each tick after it, a second hit) drops nothing more;
  - a retry clearing the living drops nothing;
  - enemies are not downable, so there is no "downed" drop.

  The pickup keeps the rounds left in the magazine at death, lying where the body fell and facing the way it faced.
- **Identity.** A pickup's netId comes from its own band, 12288–16383. The enemy band's limit moved down from 16384 to 12288, which still leaves 10,288 enemy ids. The band sits under projectiles, so a pickup costs two bytes, like an enemy. An id is never reused within a session. With the band spent, nothing more drops: a bounded rail no mission comes near.
- **Replication** (protocol 40):
  - each pickup is an entity with `Transform` and the new `Pickup` component (`weapon`: 3 bits, a WEAPON_IDS index; `ammo`: 7 bits), appended as component 12;
  - they come and go in the snapshot like projectiles, and a late joiner's first snapshot carries them;
  - only the session makes them; no client message can create one.
- **Bounded lifecycle:**
  - each goes after 120 s;
  - past 24 the oldest goes;
  - a checkpoint retry or restart clears the ground (`resetMissionWorld`);
  - a session's end is the end of its pickups, since nothing persists.
  - **Distance:** every pickup is sent to every client. The cap bounds it, and there is no per-client interest management in this protocol. It is drawn at any distance the scene draws.
- **Page:**
  - `NetClient.pickups()` reads them;
  - `PickupModels` draws each as its weapon's enemy model, laid on its side where it fell, and removes it when the host stops sending it;
  - the in-page session (`LocalServer`, the same `Session`) and a hosted room behave identically.

### Verification

- **`client/src/weapons/pickupSync.test.ts`:** a real `Session`, real `NetClient`s over loopback, and `PickupModels`.
  - **Drops:**
    - a rifleman with 17 rounds dies: one carbine pickup with 17 rounds where it fell, its netId in the band;
    - 30 ticks later there is still exactly one, with the same id;
    - an MG enemy's LMG drops nothing, and a fresh rifleman's drop has a full magazine.
  - **Replication and drawing:**
    - both the first client and a late joiner see the same pickup (id, weapon, rounds and place);
    - the page draws it at its place;
    - after 120 s it is gone on the host and both clients, and the drawing goes with it.
  - **Bounds:**
    - 29 deaths leave 24, with the 5 oldest gone;
    - 29 distinct ids;
    - a restart clears the ground, and the next drop takes an id never used before.
- **`shared/src/sim/pickups.test.ts`:**
  - the rules parse, with the LMG excluded by the documented policy;
  - bad data is refused by name;
  - the netId bands (enemies, then pickups, then projectiles) do not overlap;
  - every weapon index and droppable magazine fits the wire;
  - the component's fields are in order.
- Protocol pins moved to 40.
- `pnpm verify`: 200 files, 2138 tests, all passed.
- `pnpm bot --count 2 --ticks 600`: OK.
- **Not done:** a visual check of a pickup in the page. There is no headless camera control to frame one. The owner's look at the grey-box weapon on the ground, in the in-page session and a hosted room, is open.
