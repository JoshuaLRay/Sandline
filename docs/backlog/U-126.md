# U-126 — Give the tank a modelled, painted look instead of stacked boxes

Epic: **Mission / vehicle combat**. Status, priority, dependencies and branch/PR live in [BACKLOG.md](../../BACKLOG.md).
Source: owner report, 2026-10-05: “Improve the look of the tank. It looks like Roblox at the moment.” The same message asks for the improvement, so it is the implementation request (report-feedback step 6).

## Problem / outcome

**Observed (owner).** The tank looks like Roblox. **What is on screen:** U-070's code-built stand-in, `client/src/character/tankModel.ts`. It is a flat-coloured Lambert hull box, two track boxes, a turret box and an eight-sided barrel that ends about at the hull's nose. There are no wheels, slopes, texture or detail. U-070 called it a placeholder and said that real art comes later. `docs/art/direction.md` names this look as what the game is not: "It is not Roblox or Minecraft: no boxes for bodies".

**Wanted.** A tank that reads as a period vehicle at the 2002-console bar set by `docs/art/direction.md` and ADR-020. That means a generic ex-Soviet-style medium tank of the period, as an Afghan armed group of 2001–2002 would field one, with no real markings. It should have a modelled silhouette (sloped glacis, road wheels, sprocket and idler, tracks, fenders, a cast turret, a long gun), a painted and weathered diffuse, and smooth filtering. The gameplay should not change.

## Scope

- Add a generated asset authored as code (ADR-018 option A) under `packages/tools/src/art/vehicles/`. It should have one painted atlas and one material. The turret is its own node so it can turn apart from the hull. Run it through `gen:art` and `gen:assets` like every other generated piece.
- The client draws the generated tank when it has loaded. It keeps U-070's behaviour: the turret follows the replicated yaw, the lock lamp gives the tell, the muzzle flashes, and a dead tank becomes a darkened wreck. The hull and turret are what the aim ray hits. The code-built box model stays as the fallback if the asset fails to load ("never a blank scene").
- Size it from the archetype's `vehicle` block, so what you see and the hit capsules agree. Leave the hitbox, the muzzle data and the server unchanged.
- **Proposed defaults, which the owner can overrule:**
  - a `vehicle` budget class (8,000 triangles, 0 bones, 1 material, one 1024² texture) next to ADR-013's other proposed class rows;
  - faded olive paint under dust, not desert camouflage, because the enemy's tank is old ex-Soviet stock;
  - a drawn barrel that runs past the data muzzle, so the gun has a real overhang (the flash is drawn at the drawn tip; the shell still spawns at the data muzzle, inside the barrel);
  - turning road wheels and an engine sound are out of scope.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] A generated tank asset exists. It is committed exactly as its generator writes it, it is within its budget class, it sits within the hull and turret capsules' bounds (the gun aside), and its turret is a separate node.
- [x] In play, the client draws the generated tank. The turret follows its yaw, and the tell, the flash and the wreck still show. The hull and turret can still be shot.
- [x] If the asset is missing, the client falls back to the U-070 stand-in, never to nothing.
- [x] Renders of the new tank are captured for review. **The owner's verdict on the look is pending and is not claimed.**

## Verification

Tools tests: determinism, budget, node names, fit inside the capsules, committed source equals generator. Client tests: the asset path and the fallback path. Also `pnpm verify`, `gen:art` / `gen:assets` / `check:assets` / `check:packs`, and review renders. Visual acceptance belongs to the owner.

## Handoff / evidence

Implemented on `task/U-126-tank-art`. Its PR is linked from BACKLOG.md. It is **REVIEW: the owner's verdict on the look is pending and is not claimed.**

**What changed.**
- `tools/src/art/vehicles/` (new; ADR-018 option A) builds `vehicle-tank` from `enemies.json` → `tank.vehicle`.
  - `mesh.ts` is a hard-surface builder: prisms, lathes with an angle-dependent plan, oriented boxes and a mirror and turn stack. It winds faces to their normals and shares vertices.
  - `tank.ts` is the model. The hull has a long upper glacis, a short lower glacis and an engine deck with two louvred panels. There are fenders with three fuel tanks on the right, and tool and stowage boxes and an exhaust on the left. It also has headlamps, tow cables, hooks, spare links on the glacis, the driver's hatch and periscopes, and an unditching log. Each side has five 14-sided road wheels, an idler, a 13-tooth double sprocket, and a track of about 70 separate links. The track wraps the idler and sprocket and rests on the wheel tops, sagging between them. The turret is a low cast dome (22 sides) bulging toward the gun, with a commander's cupola, a loader's hatch carrying a heavy MG, a searchlight, a snorkel and a tarpaulin. The gun has a canvas dust cover, a fume extractor and a muzzle lip, and overhangs the nose by 1.45 m.
  - `atlas.ts` paints one 1024² diffuse:
    - three projected paint sheets (side, top, front) of faded olive under dust, thick low and on the fenders, with rain streaks, chips, cast texture on the turret and an AO ring round it;
    - the dome painted as one piece from the top sheet, so no seam crosses it;
    - sixteen cells: track link, wheel face and tyre, sprocket, steel, louvres, canvas, lens, wood, cable, bore and others.
  - `index.ts` writes the root hull node with its child node `turret` at the pivot, `sandline.class: vehicle`. `turret` is a bare pivot, and its mesh hangs under it as `turret-mesh`.
    - **Found during verification:** the pipeline's quantization gives a mesh node a decode scale and offset. With the mesh on `turret` itself, the shipped copy's turret sat at `[0, 2.10, 1.73]` × 2.62 and would have turned about a point 1.7 m ahead of the pivot.
    - A test now reads the committed web copy and fails on that layout. It reproduced the problem before the fix and passes after it.
- `budgets.json` gets a proposed `vehicle` class (8,000 triangles, 0 bones, 1 material, one 1024² texture), the same way the prop, weapon and kit rows were proposed. The owner can overrule it. Two tests that used `vehicle` as their example of an *unknown* class now use `aircraft`; the check itself is unchanged.
- `packs.json`: `vehicle-tank` joins the initial pack, because any mission script can spawn a tank.
- Client:
  - `character/vehicleAssets.ts` loads every `vehicle` asset as Lambert on one shared material, as the weapons are loaded.
  - `tankModel.ts` draws the generated model when one has been provided. The turret node turns with the eased yaw. The lamp sits on the dome's crown and the flash at the drawn muzzle. A wreck swaps in a charred Lambert that keeps the atlas, shared and never touching the template.
  - A tank already drawn is redrawn when the asset arrives or goes.
  - U-070's boxes remain the fallback. `?codetank` forces them for comparison (`docs/COMMANDS.md`).
- `docs/art/direction.md` has a new Vehicles section.

**Numbers.** 5,688 triangles (hull 4,494, turret 1,194). Two draw calls. Source 1.99 MB; web copy 1.23 MB (UASTC KTX2 and meshopt). The initial pack stays within ADR-013's 80 MB (`pnpm check:packs`).

**Not here / follow-ups.**
- Road wheels do not turn and the track does not scroll while the tank drives.
- There is no engine sound (U-070's follow-up).
- The data muzzle is unchanged (`[0, 2.2, 3.0]`), so the shell starts about 1.35 m inside the drawn barrel. Moving it to the drawn tip would be a gameplay data change, for the owner to decide.
- The gun's 2.2 m height in the data is why the dome is taller and the hull roof higher than a real T-55's.
