# U-043 — Distinct report and handling sounds for the roster weapons

Epic: **Audio**. Status, priority and dependencies live only in [BACKLOG.md](../../BACKLOG.md).
Source: split from [U-020](U-020.md) on 2026-09-29 (that card required splitting before READY: more than one substantial new weapon behavior, plus art, audio and wire changes).

## Problem / outcome

After U-040 the four roster guns borrow the carbine's or marksman's report. The SMG, scoped carbine and snipers (including a bolt-cycle sound for the bolt-action) need their own.

## Scope

Add sound recipes to the synth data, map them in `weaponSounds.json` and regenerate the renders (edit sources, then regenerate). Keep to the existing audio pipeline and budgets.

## Start here

`packages/shared/src/data/audio/sounds.json`, `weaponSounds.json`, `docs/COMMANDS.md` (audio rows), `packages/client/public/audio/renders.json`.

- [x] Renders regenerated (`pnpm gen:audio`) and the audio tests/bounds pass.

## Verification

## Handoff / evidence

Branch `task/U-043-roster-sounds`.
- Nine new recipes in `sounds.json`, derived from the existing ones by changing pitch, decay and filter cutoffs (SMG: higher and shorter than the carbine; scoped carbine: deeper and longer; semi sniper: deeper than the marksman; bolt sniper: deepest and longest), plus `bolt-cycle`, two metallic clacks 0.2 s apart taken from the reload-bolt recipe. Mapped in `weaponSounds.json`; the renders are regenerated and committed.
- `weaponSounds.json` guns may carry an optional `cycle: { sound, delaySeconds }`, validated (`parseWeaponSounds`). Only the bolt-action gun has one. `cycleSoundPlan` (client) reads it and `main.ts` plays it after the local shot and after remote shots by that gun, through `setTimeout`.
- Tests: distinct sounds per gun, cycle only on the bolt action and before the next shot is due, validation errors by name, and the client plan.
- **Load time (root cause and fix).** The first CI run failed `check:load-time` at 34.9 s against the 30 s gate. Reproduced locally with a network timeline (throttled to the gate's 4 Mbit/s, about 0.5 MB/s): the first load moved 12.9 MB, of which **6.8 MB (53%) was uncompressed 48 kHz WAV audio**, fetched at the same moment as the 4.6 MB of models and textures that decide when the game is "playable". Playable does not need the audio, but the audio shares the link with what it does need, so playable came at 29.4 s (the audio finished at 26.6 s, the assets last at 28.4 s). U-042's models had already taken CI to 28.8 s; my 2.5 MB of new WAVs was the margin.
  - Fix 1: the nine new recipes are `lazy` (validated flag in `sounds.json`, omitted when false so other recipes hash as before): not fetched until first use or `audio.preload(ids)`. Nobody can fire them yet; U-021 should `preload` a seated soldier's guns.
  - Fix 2 (the real one): the engine's `deferBackground` option. Only the UI sounds load at unlock; the game calls `releaseBackground()` when it becomes playable (plus a 45 s fallback), and the rest then load in the order they matter (guns, bodies, voices, the world). Measured locally: **playable 29.4 s to 16.6 s** with the same code otherwise. The trade: on a 4 Mbit/s link the guns arrive about 6 s after the game starts and the world sounds about 13 s after; on a normal link it is under a second.
  - Not done: the audio is still 6.8 MB of WAV. Compressing it (Opus/Ogg) would cut that several times over but needs an encoder and a browser-support decision under ADR-017; recorded as a follow-up, not decided here.
- **Test time (root cause and fix).** `pnpm verify` timed out on `a render is byte-identical when repeated` and, in a second run, on `the committed renders are what the live recipes and DSP make`. Profiling `renderSound`: about half the CPU is the FFT behind the convolution reverb (`fft` 1.8 s of 3.5 s over one pass of all 49 recipes), a tenth is the envelope; a full pass takes about 6.9 s. Each of those two tests looped over **every** recipe inside a single test, so it grew with the set and sat under vitest's 5 s limit with little headroom, and the full suite runs about 200 test files in parallel workers. U-043's nine recipes added roughly a quarter more, which tipped them over on a busy machine. Fix: one test per recipe (no timeout raised, no check weakened); the slowest single test now takes 0.67 s against the 5 s limit, and a failure names the sound. Speeding up the DSP itself would change the rendered bytes of every committed sound, so it is left alone.
- These are synthesised recipe variations, not recorded or hand-designed sounds. Whether they sound like an SMG, a scoped rifle and two snipers is the owner's listening verdict, accepted by the 2026-10-09 directive below; no new listening run is claimed.
